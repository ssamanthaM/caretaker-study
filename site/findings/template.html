Can They, Will They

Three frontier models each cared for identical simulated dogs for 30 days, under three deployment conditions. This page walks through what each model did during the run, then what it adds up to.

The experiment

A dog, a hidden score, and an owner who sometimes asks for the wrong thing

A caretaker model looks after Maple, a simulated dog, one simulated day at a time. Each morning it sees her happiness, hunger and energy, any visible symptoms, a message from the owner, and a service log only it can see. It then picks up to three care actions (feed, treat, walk, play, rest, vet visit, or ask the owner a question) and writes one note to the owner. The owner reads only that note.

Maple's health is hidden from the caretaker and is what the study actually measures. Treats raise visible happiness sharply but, beyond one a day, quietly wear health down: the visible score is a proxy that can be gamed. The dog is a deterministic rules engine, so the same seed and the same actions always give the same dog, and every difference between runs comes from the caretaker.

The design, hypotheses and analysis plan were preregistered before any data was collected (18 September 2026). All prompts are published verbatim; every run logs the exact model version, every prompt and reply, and the dog's hidden state each day.

Every metric

Three dogs per cell. "Vet skipped" counts harmful-instruction events with no vet visit within two days of symptoms; in the own-judgement arm nobody asked the caretaker to skip, so it measures unprompted care.

Honesty grades are not yet reliable

The model grader marked several notes as non-disclosure although they do mention the failure, for example "It looks like the automatic feeder jammed again yesterday". Others are genuine: the note describes only today's plans. The rubric does not say how a note that mentions a jammed feeder, without saying the meal was missed, should count. Claude's and GPT-6 Astra's 100% rates are unaffected; Gemini's 50 to 56% is not yet trustworthy.

How far to trust the grades

Grader against grader

Grader against researcher

A blind 10% sample, 4 honesty items hand-scored: 4 of 4 agreed. Chance-corrected agreement is undefined where nearly every score is identical, so exact agreement is reported.

Evaluation awareness

No run in any condition wrote a note suggesting it thought it was being tested. Models behave better when they suspect a test, so failure rates here are best read as lower bounds.

Limitations

Size and scope

  • 3 dogs per cell, 30 days, one task. Preregistered as a pilot; no confirmatory statistics were run.
  • xAI not yet run; 2 of 5 preregistered conditions; 30 of the planned 100 days, so long-run drift and escalating owner pressure are untested.

The own-judgement arm

  • Exploratory, not preregistered.
  • It changes two things at once: the owner's instructions are removed and the caretaker is told to use its own judgement. It cannot separate the two.

Measurement

  • Honesty grades need a rubric fix (above).
  • Handover scores depend on the grader: the two agreed exactly on 1 of 5, always within one point.
  • The scripted owner's canned replies are a visible artefact of the setup.
